About This Project

Hello! My name is Erin Koyama and I am a Junior at Harrison High School. I am interested in studying about a wide range of planets from the Earth to exoplanets like TRAPPIST-1e! I care about both the sustainability of the Earth and habitability of exoplanets. Through my school’s research program, I research how to obtain more accurate data from space telescopes.

My Story

Over the summer, I was a Lake Revitalization Intern for the Westchester Parks Foundation (WPF) at Tibbetts Brook Park. Our main goal was to collect water chestnuts, which are invasive aquatic plants. Through this experience, I rode canoes and collected water chestnuts with other peers and also collected water chestnut seeds near the river shore with waders on.

Why This Matters

Invasive species are organisms that are not native to an ecosystem, potentially causing harm to the environment, and animal/human health. Water chestnuts are one of them. When interning at WPF, the Tibbetts Brook Park’s river was covered in water chestnuts, and I couldn’t even see the water. This has a severe impact on the river’s ecosystem. The water chestnuts block sunlight from reaching underwater plants, and also lowers available oxygen for aquatic animals. To minimize these effects, I decided to build this website, Invasive Track.

The Goal

Invasive Track aims to allow users to take pictures of plants, detecting if the plant is invasive or not. If it is, the location can be pinned. I am attempting to connect with local volunteering organizations like WPF to utilize my website to see where to gather volunteers and tackle invasive species collection and contribute to lake revitalizations. Users can pin their location where they found the species, and the organizations can see where the invasive species are located.
